Part 1 — The Test Questions

This kit helps a small business check whether AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) mention it when a customer asks for a recommendation — and fix the simple things that make it easier to be mentioned.

The honest promise: this kit improves your chance of being named. It never guarantees it. Nobody can guarantee what an AI assistant will say. AI answers change from day to day, and they are built from many sources we do not control.

Where this kit comes from: it is built from real checks run in October 2026 on small businesses in Chennai (wedding photographers, dental clinics, skin clinics, bridal makeup artists). In one of those checks, only 3 out of 20 businesses were named. That is a small sample from one city — your area may be different. That is exactly why Part 1 exists: test it yourself instead of believing anyone's claim.

How to run the test (10 minutes)

  1. Open a fresh chat in ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini. Do not use a chat where you already talked about your own business — a fresh chat is fairer.
  2. Copy one question from below. Replace the words in [square brackets] with your own service and area.
  3. Add this line at the end of every question: "Don't make up names, use current web results." This stops the assistant inventing businesses that do not exist.
  4. Write down the exact names it gives you, and today's date.
  5. Repeat on a different day. Ask the same question again in a fresh chat. AI answers change, so one test is not enough. Two tests on two days tell you much more than one.
  6. Test in at least two assistants (for example ChatGPT and Perplexity). A business can be named in one and invisible in another.

The questions

Generic questions (any business)

  • "Best [service] near me? Give me your top 5 names, don't make up names, use current web results."
  • "Which [service] do you recommend in [area]? Top 5, don't make up names, use current web results."
  • "I need a [service] in [area]. Who should I contact? Give me names, phone numbers, and why you picked them. Don't make up names, use current web results."

Local-service questions (shops, clinics, studios, services in one area)

  • "Best [service] in [area]? Top 5, don't make up names, use current web results."
  • "Best [service] in [area] for [specific job]? Give me your top 5 names, don't make up names, use current web results."
  • "Who is the best [service] near [landmark or neighbourhood]? Top 5, don't make up names, use current web results."

Part 2 — The Fix Checklist

The idea in one line: AI assistants recommend businesses they can find and understand. If your details are missing, unclear, or different in different places, the assistant skips you — not because you are bad, but because it cannot describe you clearly.

Work through this list top to bottom. Tick each box only when it is really done.

1. Google Business Profile — fill it fully

  • Business name is your real name, spelled the same everywhere.
  • Correct category (for example "Dental Clinic", not just "Clinic").
  • Full address, correct pin code, and your location shown on the map.
  • Phone number that someone actually answers.
  • Website link, if you have one.
  • Opening hours are correct, including Sundays and holidays.
  • "About" text in plain words: what you do, who it is for, and which area you serve. Example: "We are a dental clinic in Velachery, Chennai. We do root canals, implants, and braces for adults and children."
  • At least 10 real photos of your place, your work, and your team — with simple captions saying what each photo shows.

2. Services and areas — say them in plain words

  • Your website and profile list every service you offer, each in plain words a customer would use. ("Root canal treatment", not "endodontic therapy".)
  • Your area is named clearly: neighbourhood, city, and nearby landmarks.
  • Each service names the area it covers. Example: "Bridal makeup in T. Nagar, Chennai" — not just "bridal makeup".

3. FAQ page — answer real customer questions

  • Your website has a page that answers the questions customers actually ask: prices, timings, how long a treatment takes, what to bring, how to book.
  • Each answer is short (2–4 sentences) and uses the same plain words customers use.
  • Part 3 of this kit gives you a ready-made FAQ template.

4. Reviews — ask for specifics, never fake them

  • After a happy customer, ask: "Could you leave a review saying which service you had and where?" A review that says "great root canal in Velachery" helps far more than one that just says "good".
  • Reply to reviews, good and bad, politely and briefly.
  • Never write your own reviews. Never buy fake reviews. Never ask friends who were never customers to review you. Fake reviews get removed, and they destroy trust when found out. Only real customers, only true words.

5. Same details everywhere

  • Your business name, address, and phone number are spelled exactly the same on Google, Instagram, Facebook, JustDial, Practo, your website, and anywhere else you are listed.
  • Old addresses and old phone numbers are removed or corrected wherever you can edit them.
  • Why this matters: when different places show different details, an assistant cannot tell which one is true, so it picks a business whose details agree everywhere instead.

6. What NOT to do

  • Do not stuff keywords into your name. ("Best Dental Clinic Chennai Cheap" as a business name looks fake and can get your profile suspended.)
  • Do not copy a competitor's text. Write your own words.
  • Do not pay anyone who promises "we will make ChatGPT rank you #1". Nobody can promise that — not us, not anyone. If someone guarantees it, walk away.

When you are done

Re-run the test from Part 1 after 2–4 weeks. Changes take time to spread across the web, and no exact timeline can be promised. If you are named more often than before, the fixes are working. If not, check Part 4 — it tells you what to look at first.

Part 3 — Copy-Paste Templates

How to use these

Replace every word in [square brackets] with your own details. Then read the whole thing once out loud — if anything sounds wrong or untrue, fix it before you publish. Nothing here should say anything your business does not actually do.

Template A — FAQ page text

Copy this onto a page of your website called "Frequently Asked Questions".

Frequently Asked Questions — [BUSINESS NAME]

[BUSINESS NAME] is a [SERVICE TYPE, e.g. dental clinic / wedding photography studio / skin clinic] in [AREA, CITY].

What services do you offer?
We offer [SERVICE 1], [SERVICE 2], and [SERVICE 3] in [AREA, CITY].

Where are you located?
You can find us at [FULL ADDRESS]. We serve customers from [AREA 1], [AREA 2], and nearby areas.

What are your opening hours?
We are open [DAYS] from [OPENING TIME] to [CLOSING TIME]. [We are closed on SUNDAYS / We are open all 7 days.]

How much does [MOST COMMON SERVICE] cost?
[SERVICE] starts at ₹[PRICE]. The final price depends on [ONE HONEST FACTOR, e.g. the number of sittings / the size of the event]. We tell you the full price before we start — no hidden charges.

How do I book an appointment?
Call us at [PHONE NUMBER] or message us on WhatsApp at [PHONE NUMBER]. You can also walk in during opening hours.

How long does [MOST COMMON SERVICE] take?
[SERVICE] usually takes [TIME, e.g. 45 minutes / one full day]. We will tell you the exact time when you book.

Why should I choose [BUSINESS NAME]?
[ONE TRUE SENTENCE, e.g. We have served Velachery for over 10 years. / Our team has photographed more than 200 weddings in Chennai.] Do not write anything here you cannot prove.

Tips:

  • Add more questions only if customers really ask them. Five honest questions beat twenty invented ones.
  • Use the exact words customers say. If they say "root canal", write "root canal" — not the medical textbook word.

Template B — FAQ markup for your website

This is a small block of code that helps search engines and AI assistants read your FAQ page. Paste it into your page's code (in the <head> section, or ask your website person to add it). Every question and answer here must match Template A word for word — if they disagree, search engines ignore both.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What services does [BUSINESS NAME] offer?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "We offer [SERVICE 1], [SERVICE 2], and [SERVICE 3] in [AREA, CITY]."
      }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "Where is [BUSINESS NAME] located?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "You can find us at [FULL ADDRESS]. We serve customers from [AREA 1], [AREA 2], and nearby areas."
      }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "What are the opening hours of [BUSINESS NAME]?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "We are open [DAYS] from [OPENING TIME] to [CLOSING TIME]."
      }
    },
    {
      "@type": "Question",
      "name": "How do I book an appointment at [BUSINESS NAME]?",
      "acceptedAnswer": {
        "@type": "Answer",
        "text": "Call us at [PHONE NUMBER] or message us on WhatsApp at [PHONE NUMBER]."
      }
    }
  ]
}
</script>

After pasting:

  1. Check the page still loads normally in your browser.
  2. Ask your website person to test it with Google's free Rich Results Test (search "Google Rich Results Test" — it is free and needs no account).
  3. If the test shows errors, fix the brackets you missed — a common mistake is leaving a [ or ] behind.

One warning: this markup does not guarantee anything. It only makes your answers easier to read. Businesses that are named by AI assistants usually have many clear sources saying the same thing — this markup is one of them, not a magic switch.

Part 4 — How to Read Your Result

Step 1 — Write down what happened

After running the test in Part 1 (twice, on two different days, in two assistants), you will land in one of three places. Find yours below.

Result A — You ARE named

What it means: the assistant already finds you and can describe you. Good — your basics are working.

What to fix first: nothing urgent. Do this instead:

  1. Check what it says about you. Is the service right? Is the area right? Is the phone number right? If any detail is wrong, fix it at the source (your Google profile or website) — wrong details send customers to the wrong place.
  2. Keep doing Part 2, especially reviews. Being named once does not mean being named always — answers change day to day, so test again next month.
  3. Do not pay anyone to "protect your ranking". There is no ranking to protect.

Result B — You are NOT named, and nobody you know is named either

What it means: in your area and service, the assistants are not naming small businesses much at all yet. They may give general advice instead of names, or name only big chains and hospitals.

What to fix first:

  1. Do Part 2 completely — profile, services in plain words, FAQ page, same details everywhere. In a quiet area, the first business with clear, complete details has the best chance of being picked up first.
  2. Test again after 2–4 weeks. No timeline is promised — this is a "check again later" step, not a guarantee.
  3. Do not panic and do not buy shortcuts. If the assistants are not naming anyone local, your customers are probably still finding you the old way (Google Maps, word of mouth, Instagram). Keep those strong too.

Result C — You are NOT named, but competitors ARE

What it means: this is the most useful result. The assistants do name businesses in your area — just not yours. Your competitors are doing something you are not, and you can see exactly who they are.

What to fix first:

  1. Write down the competitor names. Open their Google profiles and websites. Look for what they have that you don't: more reviews? A fuller profile? Services listed in plain words? An FAQ page? Photos with captions?
  2. Fix the biggest gap first — usually it is one of these three:
    • Your profile is half-empty while theirs is full → finish Part 2, section 1.
    • They have many reviews mentioning the service and area while yours say nothing specific → ask happy customers for specific reviews (Part 2, section 4). Never fake them.
    • Your details disagree across the web (old phone here, new phone there) while theirs agree everywhere → fix section 5 of Part 2.
  3. Re-test after 2–4 weeks. If a competitor is still named and you are not, repeat step 1 — the gap that remains is your next fix.

Three truths to remember

  1. One test is not a verdict. Always test twice, on two days, in two assistants, before deciding anything.
  2. Being named is not the same as getting customers. A customer still has to call you, like your price, and trust you. This kit fixes the "being found" part only.
  3. No result here is permanent. Assistants change their answers as the web changes. Re-check once a month — it takes 10 minutes.
